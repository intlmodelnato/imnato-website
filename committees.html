Committees

Delegates debate a prepared agenda in the following committees, and respond to a contemporary crisis.

North Atlantic Council

The North Atlantic Council, often called the Council or the NAC, is the principal decision-making body within NATO. It brings together high-level representatives of each allied country to discuss policy or operational questions requiring collective decisions.

Political Affairs Committee

The Political Affairs Committee discusses and addresses political and regional developments of interest to the Alliance. It provides advice to the NAC on issues of political importance, including cooperation agreements, enlargement, and NATO’s relationships with international organizations.

Military Committee

The Military Committee is the senior military authority in NATO. It is the principal source of military advice to NATO’s civilian decision-making bodies, the NAC and the Nuclear Planning Group.

Nuclear Planning Group

The Nuclear Planning Group was formed in 1967 so that NATO’s non-nuclear members could be associated with Allied nuclear planning. It reviews the Alliance’s nuclear policy in light of the ever-changing security environment.

Partnerships and Cooperative Security

The Partnerships and Cooperative Security Committee is responsible for all of NATO’s activities with non-member countries. It also handles NATO’s relations with other international organizations, such as the European Union, the United Nations, the Organization for Security and Cooperation in Europe, and the African Union.

Committee on Emerging Security Challenges

The Committee on Emerging Security Challenges simulates the proceedings of the Emerging Security Challenges Division within the NATO International Staff. It deals with a growing range of non-traditional risks and challenges, including terrorism, the proliferation of weapons of mass destruction, cyber defense, energy security, and the militarization of space.
